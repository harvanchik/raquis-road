Every other Friday I chop onions, celery, carrots, fennel, and thyme, then sauté everything slowly in olive oil until the vegetables shine. I deglaze with a splash of apple cider and ladle the mixture into wide-mouthed jars. Once cooled, the jars slip into the freezer where they wait like little insurance policies against illness, hard days, or unexpected company.

When someone texts that they're under the weather, I thaw a jar, add broth, shredded chicken, and cooked farro. Ten minutes later I have two quarts of fragrant soup ready for delivery. Keeping a comfort soup starter on hand means I'm prepared to serve without scrambling, and it reminds me that hospitality can be as simple as a stocked freezer and an open heart.
