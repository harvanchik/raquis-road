Some nights supper is simply soup and biscuits, but the biscuits still get to feel special. I melt salted butter until it turns hazelnut brown, then brush it over the cast-iron skillet so every edge crisps. Self-rising flour, cultured buttermilk, and a drizzle of honey come together with a wooden spoon—no cutter, no fuss. I pat the dough into the skillet and score it so the squares pull apart after baking.

The buttery aroma is always the first thing to calm the room. Kids set the table, my husband stirs the soup, and I whisper gratitude for a recipe that keeps pace with our life. When the biscuits emerge, I brush them with the remaining brown butter and sprinkle flaky salt. Each square is tender on the inside, toasted on top, and perfect for soaking up leftover broth or jam. Busy evenings deserve softness too, and these biscuits deliver it quickly.
